PAGC, the Principle of Ancestral Generative Compression, depends in this archive on a foundation count commonly stated as a 27-base by 8-modifier matrix. The local evidence, however, records competing 26, 27, 28, and 216-token formulations. We audit the repo-local primary source artifacts for the Nwagu Aneke script, extract count-bearing observations, gate downstream claims, and package the result as a reproducible negative-result manuscript. The main result is a layered count: the source chart supports 26 printed consonant rows and 8 vowel columns, while 27 bases can be recovered only as a derived split of the combined f/v row. Therefore, exact-27, 216-token, and E6 claims cannot be used as source-observed positive theory claims. The contribution is a claim-gated correction method and a concrete correction of PAGC foundation-count drift. The limitation is that the audit uses repo-local artifacts and does not replace independent philological review of the full manuscript corpus.
