# External Prior-Art Audit: ARTICLE-NA-004

## Scope

This audit supports the logograph lead-set article. The article's result is
narrow: a 30-item visible chart lead set can be audited as a seed for future
whole-word sign review, not as a complete logograph corpus.

## Compared Literatures

1. African writing-system documentation: Azuonye, Omniglot, ScriptSource, the
   Smithsonian record, and the Unicode African scripts update establish the
   Nwagu Aneke script as a documentation object, but they do not independently
   verify the 30 lead items as a completed corpus.
2. Source-critical digital humanities: TEI, IIIF, Web Annotation, PROV-O,
   RO-Crate, CIDOC CRM, DataCite, FAIR, and CARE provide ways to represent
   source, transcription, derivation, uncertainty, packaging, and governance.
   The local contribution is a lead-set rule: visible whole-word signs may be
   indexed while remaining unpromoted beyond the reviewed evidence.
3. Script encoding practice: Unicode and Script Encoding Initiative guidance
   distinguish evidence gathering from repertoire proposal. The lead set can
   inform later repertoire work, but it does not satisfy character proposal,
   naming, usage, or community-review requirements.
4. Claim verification and citation-grounded generation: SciFact and ALCE-like
   methods motivate strict evidence-to-claim alignment. The article applies that
   alignment to whole-word sign claims, especially unknown or ambiguous glosses.
5. Computational modeling: tokenization and segmentation literature shows why
   inventories matter downstream. A lead-set error can become a false lexical,
   interface, or tokenization assumption if it is treated as a finished corpus.

## Novelty Claim Ceiling

The article may claim a bounded lead-set audit and uncertainty taxonomy for the
current local chart transcription. It may not claim a complete logograph corpus,
public glyph dataset, complete semantic inventory, or community-authorized
release.

## Submission-Review Prior-Art Decision

`logograph_leadset_comparison.md` now records the non-human comparison against
cultural-heritage documentation, digital epigraphy/source-critical editions,
lexicographic corpus-building, TEI/IIIF/Web Annotation infrastructure, and
CARE/rights-aware heritage practice. Before external submission, a human
domain/source reviewer should still compare the public wording against African
script documentation and the target venue's expectations. That remaining item is
a human sign-off gate, not an unresolved technical-evidence blocker.
