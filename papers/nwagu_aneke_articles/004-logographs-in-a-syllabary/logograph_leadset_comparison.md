# Logograph Lead-Set Comparison

Article: `ARTICLE-NA-004`

Status: `COMPARISON_COMPLETE_FOR_SUBMISSION_REVIEW_HUMAN_SOURCE_SIGNOFF_BLOCKED`

## Purpose

The logograph article must be positioned as a cultural-heritage documentation
and corpus-preparation method, not as a completed lexical or glyph corpus. This
comparison resolves the non-human positioning gap while leaving source,
semantic, rights, and authority decisions to human reviewers.

## Comparison Anchors

| Anchor | What it supports | Boundary for ARTICLE-NA-004 |
|---|---|---|
| African writing-system documentation | Public and scholarly sources identify Nwagu Aneke as a documented writing-system object with whole-word sign discussion. | They do not verify the 30 visible leads as a complete corpus. |
| Cultural-heritage documentation | Lead sets can help organize future source review and preservation work. | A lead set is a review queue and indexing seed, not a public release. |

| TEI, IIIF, and Web Annotation | Infrastructure can represent source links, selectors, annotations, and uncertainty. | Standards can carry the lead set but cannot supply source authority or rights clearance. |
| CARE and rights-aware heritage practice | Public cultural-heritage data requires authority, context, and governance. | The paper must keep public source-image, glyph-crop, and culturally sensitive claims blocked until human review. |

## What This Comparison Adds

The paper's contribution is a bounded lead-set audit:

- 30 visible whole-word-sign leads are indexed from the current chart
  transcription;
- 5 leads remain unknown or uncertain;
- semantic-domain tags are provisional indexing metadata;
- the lead set is kept outside the 26 x 8 syllabic CV count;
- the result is a corpus-building seed, not a complete logograph corpus.

## Remaining Human Review

The comparison does not complete:

- human confirmation that all 30 leads are visible in the source/transcription;
- semantic review of unknown, ambiguous, or culturally sensitive glosses;
- rights clearance for public source images or glyph crops;
- cultural/source authority approval for public wording;
- review of whether any lead should be excluded from public discussion.

Those remain human sign-off gates. They do not prevent submission-review
candidate status because the proposed article is text-only and presents the lead
set as a review queue rather than a finished corpus.

## Decision

The logograph lead-set comparison is sufficient for bounded submission-review
candidate status if the target venue is asked to evaluate a cultural-heritage
documentation method and uncertainty taxonomy, not a completed lexical,
glyph-level, or public-release corpus.
