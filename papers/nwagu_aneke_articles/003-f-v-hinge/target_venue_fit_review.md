Digital Scholarship in the Humanities is a plausible target because the paper
is best framed as a digital-humanities source/derived modeling problem: how a
small orthographic transformation can be useful for computation while remaining
separate from the source-observed record.

metrics on its Oxford Academic page.

## Fit Assessment

This article fits DSH only if framed as:

- a source-critical digital-humanities method note;
- an orthographic modeling boundary for an underdocumented script;
- a reproducible rule separating source-observed and derived inventory counts;
- a safeguard against layer promotion in computational writing-system work.

## Non-Fit Boundaries

The article is not currently a fit if presented as:

- a completed Nwagu Aneke phonological account;
- a public glyph corpus;
- a Unicode proposal;
- a source-image release;
- a claim that 216 cells are source-observed;
- a broad theory of Igbo orthography.
