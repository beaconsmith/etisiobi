# External Prior-Art Audit

Article: `ARTICLE-NA-008`

Status: `PRIOR_ART_AUDIT_COMPLETE_HUMAN_SIGNOFF_BLOCKED`

## Prior-Art Areas Checked

1. **Encoded African syllabaries.** Vai, Bamum, and Mende Kikakui provide
   mature public comparators because Unicode documentation records them as
   encoded scripts with standardization treatment.
2. **African script proposal and update records.** Unicode African-script update
   materials record proposal leads, review status, and implementation signals
   for scripts such as Kpelle, Loma, New Nsibidi, and Ndebe.
3. **ScriptSource and public script-status sources.** Public script-status
   records position Nwagu Aneke as not encoded and not on the roadmap in the
   current evidence package.
4. **Digital-humanities source criticism.** TEI, IIIF, Web Annotation,
   provenance, RO-Crate, FAIR, and CARE standards support structured evidence
   packaging but do not themselves promote a source dossier into a standards
   proposal.
5. **Computational analogy risk.** Tokenization and low-resource writing-system
   modeling literature can inform downstream experiments, but computational
   usefulness is not the same as source-reviewed standardization readiness.

## Novelty Boundary

The article does not claim to discover the comparator scripts or invent Unicode
standardization practice. Its novelty is a local comparative evidence-condition
matrix that prevents typological analogy from becoming hidden readiness
promotion for Nwagu Aneke.

## Human Review Still Required

A human source/standards reviewer must confirm that the comparator selection,
standardization signals, and public wording are accurate before any external
submission.
