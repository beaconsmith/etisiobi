# GOAL-FRONTIER-001: Nwagu Aneke Open Frontier Research Ecosystem

## Outcome

Establish Etisiobi as the most rigorous, inventive, and externally legible
research platform working around Nwagu Aneke.

Transform the Nwagu Aneke research program into an expandable artifact-and-
systems laboratory capable of producing multiple forms of frontier work:

- source-critical scholarship;
- manuscript and provenance reconstruction;
- script analysis;
- computational representations;
- reproducible datasets and benchmarks;
- preservation infrastructure;
- encoding and interoperability tools;
- educational systems;
- public exhibitions and interactive archives;
- AI and agent-system experiments;
- visual, industrial, architectural, and interface design;
- community-governed cultural infrastructure;
- standards proposals;
- software products and research prototypes;
- new scientific and design questions that do not yet have established fields.

The program must not be constrained to one benchmark, one publication, one
discipline, one application, or one theory of the script.

Its purpose is to create an environment in which the strongest outcomes can
emerge through evidence, experimentation, collaboration, and contact with
reality.

The governing principle is:

```text
Exploration may be expansive.
Claims may not outrun evidence.
```

## North Star

Answer the larger question:

```text
What becomes possible when Nwagu Aneke is treated simultaneously as a
historical source, cultural artifact, writing system, formal symbolic object,
computational substrate, design language, and generator of new research
questions, while preserving provenance, uncertainty, rights, and authority?
```

The program should investigate not only what can be known about the source
layer, but also:

- what can be reconstructed;
- what remains uncertain;
- what can be formally represented;
- what can be experimentally tested;
- what can be digitally preserved;
- what can be taught;
- what can be responsibly transformed;
- what can be computationally simulated;
- what new tools can be created;
- what new design systems can emerge;
- what new scientific questions the artifact makes visible;
- what forms of public and community value can be generated.

## Strategic Thesis

Nwagu Aneke should not be reduced to:

- a chart;
- a count controversy;
- a benchmark fixture;
- a typography exercise;
- a historical curiosity;
- a single computational analogy;
- or a single product architecture.

The frontier opportunity is to construct a layered research ecosystem in which:

```text
source reconstruction
+ cultural authority
+ formal analysis
+ computation
+ preservation
+ design experimentation
+ public scholarship
+ reproducibility
-> cumulative frontier capacity
```

The program becomes gamechanging when each artifact strengthens the others.

For example:

- a source dossier improves the dataset;
- the dataset improves computational experiments;
- computational experiments reveal ambiguities;
- ambiguities guide further source research;
- provenance infrastructure makes results reviewable;
- interface design makes distinctions publicly legible;
- responsible applications test whether the research survives real use;
- external feedback produces new source, methodological, and design questions.

## Core Epistemic Architecture

The program operates through two simultaneous but non-collapsing lanes.

### Evidence Lane

Determines what may responsibly be claimed about the artifact:

- directly observed features;
- primary-source evidence;
- secondary-source claims;
- provenance;
- transcription status;
- reconstruction uncertainty;
- rights and authority;
- manuscript evidence;
- contradiction maps;
- falsification conditions.

### Frontier Lane

Determines what may be discovered from the artifact:

- latent structures;
- abstract roles;
- operations;
- transformations;
- invariants;
- generative rules;
- mathematical objects;
- computational models;
- cognitive mechanisms;
- social mechanisms;
- natural-system analogues;
- design primitives;
- product primitives;
- research programs.

The Evidence Lane must not suppress speculative discovery.

The Frontier Lane must not rewrite speculation as artifact fact.

Exploration is unrestricted.

Promotion is evidence-gated.

All records, claims, experiments, and applications should remain separable into
at least four layers:

| Layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Source-observed | Directly visible or explicitly supported by an identified source |
| Derived | Produced through a documented transformation, split, count, transcription, or inference |
| Speculative | A hypothesis, analogy, proposed model, or exploratory interpretation |
| Applied | A design, benchmark, product, system, artwork, interface, or experiment built from one or more prior layers |

Additional layers may be introduced where useful:

| Extended layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Community-attested | Supported by an identified community authority or knowledge holder |
| Computationally-generated | Produced by software, AI, simulation, clustering, or formal transformation |
| Experimentally-supported | Tested under a documented protocol with reproducible evidence |
| Contested | Supported and challenged by credible competing interpretations |
| Restricted | Known to exist but limited by rights, consent, privacy, or access conditions |

No layer is inherently inferior.

Speculation and application are permitted and encouraged when clearly labeled.
The objective is not to suppress imagination. It is to preserve the path from
imagination back to its evidential foundations.

## Claim Classes

Every substantive output must declare its class:

- `[OBS]` directly observed from the artifact;
- `[SRC]` supported by an identified source;
- `[INV]` supported by the current inventory;
- `[DERIVED]` produced through an explicit analytic operation;
- `[FORM]` formal abstraction;
- `[MATH]` mathematical model or proposition;
- `[COMP]` computational model or experiment;
- `[COG]` cognitive hypothesis;
- `[SOC]` social or institutional hypothesis;
- `[BIO]` biological comparison;
- `[CHEM]` chemical comparison;
- `[ANALOGY]` cross-domain resemblance;
- `[SPEC]` open speculation;
- `[TEST]` falsifiable or experimentally testable hypothesis;
- `[NEW]` newly proposed research object, method, or program;
- `[NEG]` negative or null result;
- `[DROP]` discarded branch.

No discipline, comparison, application, or formal representation is prohibited
at the exploration stage.

Only unsupported promotion is prohibited.

## Research Portfolio

### Lane 1: Source-Critical Reconstruction and Manuscript Archaeology

Build the strongest possible record of the source environment:

- Azuonye source trail and citation status;
- Omniglot chart provenance and limitations;
- manuscript and exercise-book search paths;
- library, archive, family, institutional, and private-collection leads;
- edition and reproduction history;
- date, authorship, and transmission questions;
- observed row and column inventory;
- ambiguity ledger for uncertain glyphs;
- version history across reproduced charts;
- image-quality and transcription risk;
- rights, consent, custody, and authority status;
- oral-history opportunities where appropriate;
- evidence maps connecting each claim to its source.

Possible outputs include a critical source dossier, annotated digital facsimile,
manuscript census, provenance graph, source comparison atlas, documentary
research paper, or rights-aware preservation package.

### Lane 2: Community Knowledge, Authority, Rights, and Benefit

Treat authority and cultural responsibility as a research domain rather than a
final administrative check.

Explore who has standing to interpret, reproduce, teach, license, or
commercialize; community knowledge absent from published sources; attribution
and naming protocols; consent models; data sovereignty; appropriate access
tiers; benefit-sharing; co-authorship and community review; culturally
meaningful preservation; public-release risk; and governance models for future
archives, datasets, or products.

### Lane 3: Count Layers, Variants, and Structural Audit

Make distinctions such as 26/208 and derived 27/216 impossible to collapse.

Develop source-observed count ledgers, derived split ledgers, variant and
allograph ledgers, contradiction maps, affected-claim indexes, transformation
histories, alternative count models, reviewer-facing falsification conditions,
machine-readable layer labels, visual count-model interfaces, and sensitivity
tests showing which conclusions change under each model.

The objective is larger than resolving one count. This lane can become a
general method for preventing derived data from silently becoming historical
fact.

### Lane 4: Script Structure, Linguistic Analysis, and Formal Description

Treat the script as an artifact and system before fixing it to one theory.

Investigate glyph and syllable inventories; logographs and their source status;
phonetic assignments; vowel or modifier behavior; ordering and placement; row
and column semantics; allography; ligature possibilities; display conventions;
matrix-as-pedagogy versus matrix-as-system; reading direction;
compositionality; semantic compression; redundancy and error correction;
modifier order; modifier algebra; and relationships among glyph form, sound,
function, and context.

Comparative work is allowed at any stage when explicitly identified as
comparative or speculative rather than source-observed.

### Lane 5: Digital Representation, Encoding, and Interoperability

Explore how the script can be represented without flattening uncertainty.

Investigate canonical identifiers; image, vector, and structured
representations; fonts; keyboards; transliteration layers; Unicode feasibility;
Private Use Area prototypes; variant encoding; glyph composition models;
machine-readable uncertainty; links between encoded signs and source images;
export formats; APIs; accessibility; digital preservation risks; and rendering
across devices and operating systems.

### Lane 6: Computational Experiments and Formal Models

Use computation to generate and test questions, not merely to decorate the
research.

Possible experiments include tokenizer baselines; compression and
description-length studies; graph representations; finite-state and grammar
models; modifier composition tests; sequence and order sensitivity; glyph-form
clustering; computer-vision similarity studies; uncertainty-aware OCR;
information-density comparisons; error-correction models; generative
reconstruction; simulation of alternative count systems; representation
learning; symbolic-neural hybrid models; human-versus-machine transcription;
and negative, null, and failed results as first-class artifacts.

Computational models must record whether they operate on source images,
source-observed transcriptions, derived transcriptions, synthetic data,
speculative models, or application-layer abstractions.

### Lane 7: AI, Agents, and Research-Integrity Systems

Use Nwagu Aneke as a hard case for testing how AI handles layered, uncertain,
culturally sensitive evidence.

Possible directions include LPE-Bench, evidence-preservation benchmarks,
layer-confusion tests, hallucination and citation audits, agentic source
reconstruction, claim-to-source tracing, uncertainty calibration, review agents,
multi-agent disagreement, source/derived/speculative classification,
research-memory systems, provenance-aware retrieval, AI-assisted transcription
with human adjudication, structured refusal when evidence is insufficient, and
comparison of different model and agent frameworks.

LPE-Bench, Inspect AI, MLAgentBench, and related tools may remain supporting
instruments or become central if results demonstrate that they offer the
strongest frontier contribution.

No tool is privileged or excluded in advance.

### Lane 8: Provenance, Reproducibility, and Open Research Infrastructure

Make every defensible branch independently inspectable.

Develop RO-Crate or equivalent metadata packaging; DataLad or equivalent
dataset versioning; software provenance records; Software Heritage deposits;
cryptographic checksums; versioned count-model changes; reproducible
environments; containerized experiments; decision traces; claim graphs;
non-sensitive fixtures; rights-aware access controls; machine-readable methods;
uncertainty and contradiction logs; experiment registries; reproducible
negative results; and independent reconstruction challenges.

### Lane 9: Interfaces, Visualization, Education, and Public Understanding

Create interfaces that allow people to understand both the script and the
evidence boundaries around it.

Explore interactive glyph atlases; layered claim viewers; source-versus-derived
comparison tools; educational games; classroom kits; visual timelines;
provenance maps; manuscript exploration environments; AR or spatial interfaces;
tactile and accessible representations; animated modifier demonstrations;
public lectures; exhibitions; documentary storytelling; multilingual learning
resources; and curator or teacher tools.

### Lane 10: Generative Design and Creative Systems

Treat Nwagu Aneke as a source of design questions without presenting
application-layer inventions as historical facts.

Explore visual identity systems; generative graphics; type and motion design;
symbolic interfaces; industrial design; architecture; material patterning;
robotics; wearables; musical systems; narrative and worldbuilding; information
visualization; spatial wayfinding; modular product languages; physical
computing; interactive installations; modifier-governed creative tools.

Applications must preserve lineage from source observation through abstraction
to design output.

### Lane 11: Cross-Domain Scientific and Systems Research

Explore whether formal features abstracted from the script illuminate broader
systems without claiming that those systems historically derive from it.

Possible domains include molecular decoding, epigenetic regulation, neural
attention, memory systems, cryptographic policy, access control, semantic
routing, adaptive materials, swarm coordination, ecological signaling, language
design, information theory, category theory, dynamical systems,
human-computer interaction, governance and law, programmable disclosure, agent
permissions, and user-owned cognition.

The research question is:

```text
Which abstract operations remain useful when transported across domains, and
which properties fail under translation?
```

### Lane 12: Products, Standards, Partnerships, and Sustainable Research

Explore how research artifacts can become durable public infrastructure.

Possibilities include research software, archives, datasets, APIs, fonts,
keyboards, benchmark platforms, cultural products, educational services,
exhibitions, preservation services, standards proposals, grants and
institutional partnerships, fellowships, open-source communities,
community-governed licensing, mission-aligned commercial products, research
residencies, and international scholarly collaborations.

Commercial or product activity is permitted when source and application layers
remain distinguishable, rights and authority have been reviewed, attribution is
explicit, benefits and risks are considered, and unsupported historical claims
are not used as marketing.

The program should keep questions open rather than prematurely choosing a
single explanatory model.

Examples include:

1. What is directly observable in the surviving source layer?
2. Which current representations are faithful reproductions, pedagogical
   reconstructions, or later derivations?
3. Does the visible matrix represent the script itself, a teaching device, a
   charting convention, or multiple functions at once?
4. What is the strongest defensible interpretation of the modifier structure?
5. Which ambiguities can be resolved through source work, and which should
   remain explicitly unresolved?
6. What encoding model preserves form, sound, uncertainty, provenance, and
   variant status?
7. Can the script support measurable compression or compositionality claims?
8. Can layered-claim infrastructure reduce AI hallucination in cultural
   heritage research?
9. Which computational representations generate useful tests rather than
   aesthetic analogies?
10. What public interfaces make observation, derivation, speculation, and
    application intuitively distinguishable?
11. What design systems can emerge without retroactively assigning invented
    meanings to historical forms?
12. What new forms of user-owned memory, disclosure, interpretation, or
    semantic programming can be inspired by the modifier architecture?
13. What would independent researchers need to reproduce, challenge, or extend
    the work?
14. What does responsible prominence look like for Etisiobi and for the

## Frontier Outcome Criteria

The program reaches a frontier outcome when one or more defensible results
materially changes what can be known, done, tested, preserved, represented, or
responsibly built.

Qualifying outcomes may include:

1. A source-critical reconstruction changes what can responsibly be claimed.
2. A newly located or better-established source alters the known record.
3. A count-layer audit prevents or corrects a major false research branch.
4. A formal description reveals a previously unarticulated structural property.
5. An encoding or interoperability model preserves distinctions that existing
   representations collapse.
6. A computational experiment produces a reproducible positive, negative, or
   null result of wider significance.
7. An AI benchmark exposes a measurable failure mode in evidence-sensitive
   research.
8. A provenance system makes the research independently inspectable.
9. A community-governed preservation artifact creates durable cultural value.
10. A public interface makes epistemic layers understandable to non-specialists.
11. A responsible design application demonstrates traceable abstraction from
    source to application.
12. A software, hardware, or agent architecture operationalizes a novel modifier
    principle.
13. A standards proposal improves long-term representation or preservation.
14. A new interdisciplinary research question attracts independent validation.
15. A product or partnership sustains further research without compromising
    rights or evidence integrity.

A frontier outcome may also be a corrected or expanded sign inventory, a new
mathematical object or formal system, a theorem or counterexample, a glyph/OCR
dataset, a cognitive or educational experiment, a demonstrated false analogy,
a preservation protocol, a standards feasibility result, a product primitive,
a general method for latent-structure discovery, an interdisciplinary synthesis,
a reproducible research platform, a public visual atlas, or a portfolio of
mutually reinforcing results.

Multiple outcomes may mature in parallel. The program is not required to
collapse into a single paper-candidate branch.

## Portfolio Selection

Branches should be evaluated periodically across source strength, novelty,
falsifiability, reproducibility, community legitimacy, rights feasibility,
technical feasibility, external relevance, strategic distinctiveness, public
value, potential for independent validation, time to a defensible result, and
ability to unlock other lanes.

No branch should be promoted only because it is easy to publish. No branch
should be suppressed only because it is unconventional.

## Adaptive Readiness Gates

Readiness gates apply per branch rather than serially to the whole program.

### Gate A: Source Integrity

Required for source-facing claims:

- identifiable source basis;
- extraction or transcription method;
- uncertainty record;
- observation/derivation separation;
- contradiction and falsification path.

### Gate B: Rights and Authority

Required for restricted materials, community-facing claims, public archives,
commercial applications, or culturally sensitive release:

- rights status;
- authority status;
- consent or access conditions;
- attribution plan;
- risk and benefit assessment.

### Gate C: Methodological Integrity

Required for experimental claims:

- explicit research question;
- documented inputs;
- reproducible method;
- baseline or comparison;
- failure criteria;
- negative-result handling.

### Gate D: Technical Validation

Required for software, benchmark, dataset, or product claims:

- executable artifact;
- versioned dependencies;
- tests;
- provenance;
- known limitations;
- independent or clean-environment reproduction where possible.

### Gate E: Application Integrity

Required for design and product outputs:

- source-to-abstraction lineage;
- application-layer labeling;
- rights review;
- usability or contact-with-reality test;
- no invented application meaning presented as historical meaning.

### Gate F: External Release

Required before a branch is described as frontier-ready, paper-ready,
submission-ready, deployment-ready, or publication-ready:

- branch-specific review;
- passing relevant validators;
- evidence package;
- reviewer attack surface;
- decision trace;
- release and attribution approval.

Passing Gate F may authorize publication PDFs, preprints, conference or journal
submission, public demonstrations, exhibitions, grant applications,
partnerships, product pilots, dataset release, or standards engagement.

## Research Operating Loop

Use a continuous portfolio loop:

```text
observe
-> document
-> reconstruct
-> model
-> experiment
-> compare
-> challenge
-> translate
-> prototype
-> test in reality
-> review
-> preserve
-> publish or revise
-> generate the next question
```

Every cycle may produce stronger evidence, a contradiction, a failed
hypothesis, a new dataset, software artifact, application, public resource,
collaboration, or new research lane.

All are legitimate research outcomes when properly recorded.

## Discovery Cycle

Every major cycle must execute:

```text
artifact grounding
-> uncertainty registration
-> meaning suspension
-> role extraction
-> latent-structure generation
-> formula mining
-> competing formalizations
-> cross-domain scan
-> research-object generation
-> experiment generation
-> prior-art comparison
-> adversarial review
-> branch selection
-> child investigation
-> recursive restart
```

The cycle must not terminate because one explanation appears adequate.

It terminates only when no new significant structures emerge, no new testable
questions emerge, prior art absorbs the remaining branches, evidence is
saturated, experiments repeatedly produce null results, branch quality declines,
or marginal information gain becomes low.

## Minimum Discovery Floors

These are floors for a full frontier cycle, not ceilings or quotas. A full
cycle should attempt to generate at least:

- 30 candidate latent structures;
- 20 candidate formula families;
- 10 competing formal representations;
- 50 cross-domain comparisons;
- 10 significant research-grade objects;
- 10 falsifiable hypotheses;
- 10 experiment proposals;
- 10 article, dataset, benchmark, protocol, or product candidates;
- 20 adversarial objections;
- 3 high-information-gain child investigations.

There is no upper limit. Weak candidates must be discarded even if doing so
reduces the final count.

If fewer than 10 strong research objects survive, the cycle must document the
exhausted branches and launch a new extraction strategy before concluding that
the artifact does not support them.

## Research Object Standard

A candidate becomes a significant research object when it has:

- a clear definition independent of the artifact;
- identifiable supporting observations;
- explicit counterevidence;
- at least one formal representation;
- a connection to prior work;
- a test, proof, simulation, dataset, or falsification path;
- stated collapse conditions;
- value even if its original interpretation fails.

A research object should preferably be mathematically formalizable,
computationally implementable, experimentally testable, cross-domain
comparable, able to generate a benchmark, theorem, conjecture, product
primitive, dataset, negative result, or field reframing.

## Long-Term Position

The desired result is not simply a completed study.

It is a durable research ecosystem in which Nwagu Aneke can support better
historical knowledge, preservation, computational methods, research-integrity
systems, public understanding, design languages, technologies, institutions, and
questions that would not otherwise have become visible.

Etisiobi should become known not for making the loudest claim, but for creating
the richest, most reliable, and most generative environment in which the
frontier can unfold.
