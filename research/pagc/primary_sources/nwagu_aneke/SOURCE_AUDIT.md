## Source-Observed Facts (HIGH CONFIDENCE)

These are claims that multiple independent sources agree on:

1. **Type:** Syllabary with logographic supplements (Azuonye 1992, Omniglot, Ahamefula & Mbah 2011)
2. **Syllable type:** CV (consonant-vowel) syllables only (Azuonye 1992, Omniglot, Ahamefula & Mbah 2011)
3. **No standalone vowels:** "There are no symbols for independent vowels, which are not written" (Omniglot). Vowel sounds encoded only through combination with consonants (multiple sources)
4. **No tone marking:** The script does not represent lexical tones; meaning inferred from context (Ahamefula & Mbah, web sources)
5. **Logographs exist:** Characters representing complete words or morphemes supplement the CV syllables (Azuonye, Omniglot, Ahamefula & Mbah)
6. **Dialect:** Umuleri dialect of Igbo, Anambra State (all sources)
7. **Inventor:** Ogbuevi Nwagụ Aneke, c.1910–1991 (all sources)
8. **Corpus:** 100+ exercise books of diary entries, spiritual prophecies, anti-colonial commentary (Azuonye, Omniglot)
9. **Nsibidi resemblance:** Some symbols resemble Nsibidi pictographic/ideographic writing (Omniglot)
10. **Writing direction:** Left to right, horizontal lines (Omniglot)
11. **Approximate symbol count:** ~200 symbols (web search summary). NOT 216.

## ⚠️ CRITICAL FINDING: The Matrix Is a Display Convention

The web search returned this explicit statement:

> "There is no standardized 'matrix' structure (such as a fixed grid of rows and columns) for the script. While linguistic analyses or pedagogical charts may organize the symbols into rows and columns to illustrate the syllabic combinations, the script itself was used by Aneke in his notebooks as a flowing, linear system of writing, similar to standard prose."

**Implication:** The "27×8 matrix" may be an artifact of how someone (Azuonye? Omniglot?) organized the chart for display purposes, not a property of the script itself. The matrix is a *representation choice*, not a *structural fact*.

## Igbo Vowel System (Cross-Reference)

Standard Igbo has **8 vowels** organized in two harmony sets:

| Set | Vowels |
|---|---|
| +ATR (advanced tongue root) | i, u, e, o |
| −ATR | ị, ụ, ẹ (written as e in some dialects), ọ |

The Umuleri dialect, which the script encodes, may have variations. The number 8 is plausible as a column count if the chart organizes by these 8 vowels — but this must be confirmed from the Azuonye chart or Omniglot image, not assumed.

---

## Igbo Consonant System (Cross-Reference)

Standard Igbo consonant phonemes vary by analysis but typically include:

**Stops:** b, d, g, gb, k, kp, kw, gw, p, t  
**Nasals:** m, n, ṅ (ny), ñ  
**Fricatives:** f, gh, h, s, sh, z  
**Affricates:** ch, j  
**Approximants/Liquids:** l, r, w, y  

Counts vary from ~23 to ~36 depending on analysis and whether digraphs/labiovelars/palatals are counted separately. The number 27 is within the plausible range but is NOT a standard count from any published Igbo phonology.

**Critical question:** Does the Omniglot chart have exactly 27 rows? Or some other number? Only examining the chart image directly will answer this.
