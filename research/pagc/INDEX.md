## What Is PAGC?

The **Principle of Ancestral Generative Compression (PAGC)** is now treated as a hypothesis program, not an established theory. It investigates whether the Nwagụ Aneke Igbo syllabary supports a source-derived base/modifier structure and whether any such structure has measurable value for Igbo tokenization, compression, memory, or representation tasks.

The program now separates four lanes: source truth, formalization, experiment,
and transfer. Source claims remain strict; derived formal hypotheses are allowed
as modern research constructions when they define their rules, baselines, and
kill conditions.

Current formalization record:
[`DH-001: Derived 27 x 8 Completion`](hypotheses/DH-001-derived-27x8-completion.md)
is `PARKED_BY_GLOBAL_BENCHMARK_GATE` after a constructed role-recovery baseline,
a source-promotion counterexample screen, a source-record/adversarial screen,
and a global benchmark gate. It is not source evidence, a historical claim, or a
paper candidate.

Current strongest experimental branch:
[`DH-003: Layer-Promotion Error Benchmark`](hypotheses/DH-003-layer-promotion-error-benchmark.md)
is `INTERNAL_KNOWLEDGE_BREAKTHROUGH` after producing ten bounded
benchmark-design knowledge units in
`experiments/EXP-DH-003-layer-promotion-knowledge-benchmark/`. Its claim ceiling
is internal repo knowledge, not public benchmark or paper-candidate status.

Unproven working claims include:
- `27×8` as a source-derived matrix
- a lexical cache of high-frequency moral/equity terms
- surface variation tolerance
- deliberate redundancy as robustness

Origin: the **Nwagụ Aneke syllabary**, an indigenous Igbo script invented c.1958 by Ogbuevi Nwagụ Aneke of Umuleri, Anambra State, Nigeria. The source archive has started, but the repository has not yet completed symbol transcription or inventory validation.

Full background: [`paper/kb/nwagu_aneke_syllabary.md`](paper/kb/nwagu_aneke_syllabary.md)

## Principia-Level Pathways

These are the specific contributions that could be paradigm-shifting if PAGC's claims hold:

### 1. The E₆ Symmetry Claim *(Mathematics + Physics + Quantum)*
**Claim:** PAGC's 27 bases transform under the Weyl group W(E₆), making the 27×8 matrix a linguistic realization of the exceptional Lie group E₆.
**Test:** Embed 27 bases in semantic vector space; compute Gram matrix eigenvalues; compare to W(E₆) character table.
**If confirmed:** Connects African linguistics to exceptional Lie theory, string theory, and quantum gravity. A genuine Principia contribution.

### 2. The Universal Compression Claim *(Information Theory + AI)*
**Claim:** PAGC achieves minimum-description-length encoding of Igbo semantic space and generalizes to cross-domain compression (text, music, visual patterns).
**Test:** MDL comparison against UTF-8, BPE-Igbo, IPA on diverse Igbo corpus + non-text Igbo cultural data.
**If confirmed:** PAGC is a provably optimal universal compression scheme rooted in Indigenous linguistics.

### 3. The Genetic Code Isomorphism *(Synthetic Biology)*
**Claim:** PAGC's 27×8 matrix is structurally isomorphic to the genetic codon table (4×4×4 = 64 codons encoding 20+stop) at the information-theoretic level.
**Test:** Compute information-theoretic distances between PAGC and the codon table using coding theory metrics.
**If confirmed:** PAGC is a linguistic analog of the genetic code — a universal biological information principle instantiated in human language.

### 4. The Decolonial Epistemology Contribution *(Independent of technical claims)*
**Claim:** A knowledge system dismissed for 60+ years because of colonial-era origin and non-Western provenance contains insights parallel to foundational Western science.
**Impact:** Regardless of whether the technical claims hold, documenting PAGC rigorously and comparing it to Shannon/Chomsky/Lie is an epistemic justice contribution — it establishes that Indigenous knowledge systems deserve frontier-level scientific analysis.

## Key Falsification Conditions

| Claim | Falsified if |
|-------|-------------|
| 27 bases are optimal | BPE on large Igbo corpus converges on n≠27 as optimal base count |
| 8 modifiers are optimal | Modifier ablation shows no perplexity discontinuity at k=8 |
| Lexical cache is valid | RFC analysis of naturalistic Igbo corpus does not rank moral/equity terms as high-frequency |
| E₆ symmetry | PAGC's 27-base Gram matrix has different eigenvalue structure from W(E₆) representation |
| Universal compression | PAGC achieves worse MDL than UTF-8 on any Igbo text corpus |
| Octonionic modifiers | Modifier composition in Igbo is associative (A∘B = B∘A for all modifiers A,B) |

## Critical Counter-Arguments (Summary)

1. **Numerology risk:** 27, 8, and 216 have many mathematical properties. Selection of the ones that support PAGC while ignoring those that don't is confirmation bias.
2. **Origin ≠ validity:** The ancestral revelation claim is epistemologically interesting but mathematically irrelevant. The 27×8 structure must be validated against Igbo phonological data, not against the provenance story.
3. **Small corpus problem:** Aneke's 100+ exercise books are a single-author, single-dialect, single-genre corpus. Validating PAGC requires diverse, naturalistic, multi-dialect Igbo text data.
4. **Universality is an extraordinary claim:** Every discipline where PAGC claims to apply would need independent validation. Cross-domain universality requires cross-domain evidence.
5. **The lexical cache is not unique:** Any tokenizer trained on Igbo moral/equity texts would produce a similar cache. The claim that PAGC's specific cache is optimal needs comparative evaluation.
