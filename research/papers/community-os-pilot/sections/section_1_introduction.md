# Section 1: Introduction — Receipts Over Recall

Informal institutions are the "hidden scaffolding" of governance in many parts of the Global South. In Southeast Nigeria, community-led town unions provide security, maintain infrastructure, and resolve disputes where the formal state is absent or under-resourced. However, these institutions suffer from a fundamental "coordination ceiling": their reliance on social recall and informal deliberation leads to disputes, "zombie proposals," and elite capture.

This paper presents the Oroma Coordination OS, a design science artifact designed to stabilize informal coordination through an "Artifact-First" approach. We argue that by transitioning from "recall-based trust" to "receipt-based trust"—where every institutional decision is anchored in a verifiable digital record—community institutions can achieve the administrative durability of formal bureaucracies without losing their moral legitimacy.

## 1.1 Research Objective
The objective of this research is to design, implement, and formatively evaluate a digital coordination system that replicates the complex governance logic of Southeast Nigerian town unions. We ask: *How can a digital artifact provide the "institutional memory" necessary to reduce coordination failure in informal settings?*

## 1.2 The Design Science Approach
Following the Design Science Research (DSR) paradigm (Hevner et al. 2004), we developed Oroma through an iterative build-and-evaluate process. The artifact is grounded in the real-world problem environment of Nigerian community governance and is evaluated against its ability to provide durability, verifiability, and non-repudiability to informal decisions.

## 1.3 Contributions
This paper makes two primary contributions:
1. **The Oroma Artifact:** A hybrid "DB-primary, chain-legible" system architecture for community coordination.
2. **Design Principles for Informal Institutions:** A set of seven design decisions (Section 4) that bridge the gap between informal social capital and formal digital systems.

By providing a verifiable infrastructure for collective action, Oroma enables informal institutions to scale their impact and integrate more effectively with formal e-government ecosystems.
