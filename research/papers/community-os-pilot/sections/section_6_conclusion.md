# Section 6: Conclusion and Future Work

The Oroma Coordination OS represents a shift from viewing informal institutions as "low-capacity" actors to viewing them as "evidence-deprived" institutions. By providing a digital infrastructure for coordination, the artifact stabilizes the "social recall" that currently limits the durability of community governance in Southeast Nigeria.

## 6.1 Summary of Findings
Our formative evaluation (Section 5) demonstrates that the artifact successfully replicates the polycentric governance logic of town unions through its modular smart contract suite and deliberative state machines. By enforcing "receipts over recall," Oroma provides a verifiable mechanism for collective decision-making that reduces the risk of zombie proposals and information asymmetry.

## 6.2 Limitations and Ongoing Research
As an "ongoing research" paper, this work focuses on the internal validity and design logic of the artifact. The primary limitation is the lack of long-term summative data from a live field pilot. The next phase of research will involve:
1. **The Trust-Delta Pilot:** A 6-month deployment in three town unions to measure the impact on member trust and participation.
2. **Offline-First Synchronization:** Enhancing the "multi-modal" interaction layer to support coordination in environments with zero internet connectivity.

## 6.3 Final Takeaway
The "Third Tier" of governance—the community layer—is the most resilient but least digitized part of the Global South’s institutional landscape. By providing an "Artifact-First" foundation, Oroma suggests that digital public infrastructure (DPI) should not just serve the state, but should empower the community institutions that already carry the burden of governance.
