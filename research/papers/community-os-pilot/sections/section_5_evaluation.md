# Section 5: Evaluation Plan — Pre-Deployment Formative Assessment

As Oroma is a pre-deployment artifact, the evaluation follows the **formative evaluation** paradigm (Hevner et al. 2004), focusing on the internal consistency, utility, and feasibility of the design. We explicitly avoid "artificial" evaluation traps (De Sordi 2020) by centering the assessment on the artifact’s ability to represent existing community governance structures.

## 5.1 Structural Mapping Accuracy
The primary evaluation metric is the **structural mapping accuracy**: how precisely do the modular smart contracts (Section 4.2) replicate the actual roles and rules of a standard Southeast Nigerian town union constitution? This will be verified through a systematic walkthrough of the *Nnewi Town Union* and *Enugu-Ukwu* constitutions against the Oroma Registry and Program contracts.

## 5.2 The "Duty to Document" Feasibility
Following ISO 15489 principles, we assess the feasibility of the metadata requirements (TTI-01). Can a community officer realistically fulfill the 50-character purpose requirement during a high-velocity town union meeting? This will be evaluated through a "stress-test" simulation using historical meeting minutes as input data.

## 5.3 Security and Non-Repudiability
The "receipts" generated by the system (receipts-over-recall) must be technically non-repudiable. The evaluation involves verifying the cryptographic signatures for all on-chain state transitions (DD-01). Success is defined as the inability of an administrator to alter a closed meeting record without invalidating the cryptographic hash.

## 5.4 External Validity and Future Pilot Plan
While the current research focuses on the artifact’s internal validity, Section 6 outlines the plan for the **summative evaluation** (field pilot). This pilot will measure the "Trust Delta"—the difference in perceived fairness (Tyler 2006) among community members when using Oroma vs. traditional paper-based or oral recall systems.
