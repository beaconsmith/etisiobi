## 2. Working Thesis

### Current thesis
Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) — as defined by the G20, World Bank, and RIS — is conceived as national-scale, state-anchored, and governed by legal/regulatory frameworks. This paper examines whether the DPI concept can be meaningfully extended to community-layer infrastructure: payments, records, and coordination systems operated by community institutions in low-trust environments. Drawing on polycentric governance theory and evidence that community institutions already deliver physical infrastructure in the Global South, the paper asks whether "community DPI" is a coherent category or a category error.

### One-sentence contribution claim
This paper critically examines whether DPI concepts (interoperability, modularity, non-excludability) are transferable to community-scale infrastructure, proposing a taxonomy of functions without claiming equivalence to national systems.

### Why this paper exists
The DPI literature has a genuine definitional boundary: everything assumes state-level operation. But community institutions already deliver infrastructure functions (roads, water, markets). Can they deliver digital infrastructure functions too? No one has asked this question in the DPI literature. The paper's contribution is the question itself + preliminary framework.

### What would make this paper worth accepting
A reviewer should value this paper if it: (1) defines the boundary problem precisely, (2) doesn't over-claim that community systems are DPI (avoids category confusion), and (3) identifies specific DPI properties that are and aren't transferable.
