# Section 6: Conclusion — Toward a Polycentric Digital Future

The future of digital governance in the Global South does not lie in more centralized state portals, but in the stabilization of the community institutions that already hold the trust of the people. This paper has argued that Southeast Nigerian town unions are not just stakeholders to be consulted, but are "principal agents" capable of driving digital transformation.

## 6.1 Summary of the Framework
Our framework suggests that the "coordination ceiling" of informal institutions can be broken through the strategic use of digital artifacts. By providing a verifiable infrastructure for memory—"receipts over recall"—informal institutions can achieve administrative durability while maintaining their moral legitimacy. This model moves beyond state-centrism, embracing the polycentric reality of African governance.

## 6.2 The Path Forward: Middle-Out Digitalization
We propose a "middle-out" strategy for e-government, where the state provides the standards and interoperability layers (DPI) while the community institutions manage the local logic of coordination. This approach respects "User Sovereignty" and ensures that digital transformation is culturally grounded and resilient to state-level political instability.

## 6.3 Final Vision
As we look toward ICEGOV 2026 and beyond, the goal of "Digital Transformation for Success" must include the "Third Tier" of governance. When community institutions are equipped with the digital tools to anchor their own decisions, they move from being the "missing link" of development to being the engines of a truly polycentric digital future.
