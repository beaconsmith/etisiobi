# Section 2: Related Work — Beyond State-Centricity

This section critiques the prevailing state-centric paradigm in digital governance research and identifies the "informal institution gap" as a primary barrier to digital transformation in the Global South.

## 2.1 The State-Centric Bias in E-Government
Traditional e-government frameworks (e.g., e-Government Maturity Models) implicitly assume that the state is the sole provider of governance and the primary driver of digital service delivery. While successful in Western bureaucracies, this "state-centrism" often leads to digital failure in sub-Saharan Africa, where the state’s presence is "thin" or fragmented (Heeks 2017, Effoduh 2024). By focusing exclusively on state-led portals and registries, mainstream research overlooks the vital role of non-state actors who already perform significant governance functions.

## 2.2 Town Unions: The "Fourth Tier" of Governance
In Southeast Nigeria, town unions are described in the literature as the "principal agents" of community development (SFJESGS 2021). These institutions are more than just civil society organizations; they act as a de facto "Fourth Tier" of government, providing security, resolving land disputes, and building physical infrastructure (Nwosu 2009). Despite their high moral legitimacy and operational scale, town unions remain almost entirely absent from digital governance literature.

## 2.3 Informal Institutions and Hybrid Governance
Literature on "hybrid governance" suggests that the most resilient institutional arrangements in Africa are those where formal and informal rules overlap (Muwafu 2024). However, the digital tools currently used for e-government are built on formal-only assumptions. There is a critical need for research into how digital transformation can be "exapted" (Gregor & Hevner 2013) to serve the hybrid reality of community institutions.

## 2.4 The Gap: Community-Led Digital Transformation
We identify a research gap at the intersection of informal institution studies and digital public infrastructure (DPI). While the "India Stack" and similar state-led DPI projects have shown success, they do not yet account for community-led centers of decision-making. This paper fills this gap by proposing a model for community-led digital transformation that empowers existing polycentric actors rather than attempting to replace them with state bureaucracies.
