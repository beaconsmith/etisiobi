## 2. Working Thesis

### Current thesis
Mainstream digital governance research is overwhelmingly state-centric, treating government agencies as the natural unit of analysis for e-government success and digital transformation. This framing systematically overlooks community institutions — town unions, rotating savings associations, age grades, women's associations, village assemblies, and diaspora organizations — that in Southeast Nigeria perform significant governance functions including coordination, resource mobilization, dispute resolution, and collective decision-making. Drawing on polycentric governance theory (Ostrom), legitimacy theory (Tyler, Beetham, Suchman), and empirical evidence from Southeast Nigerian community development, this paper argues that these institutions should be treated as primary units of digital governance analysis, not peripheral informal actors.

### One-sentence contribution claim
This paper demonstrates that community institutions in Southeast Nigeria already perform governance functions that digital governance frameworks should measure and support, not merely bypass.

### Why this paper exists
The e-government literature has a genuine blind spot: community-layer governance is either invisible or treated as incidental. This paper fills that gap with both theoretical grounding (Ostrom, legitimacy theory) and regional evidence (town union studies, Igbo political organization). No other paper in the portfolio makes this specific descriptive/analytical argument.

### What would make this paper worth accepting
A reviewer should value this paper if it convincingly shows that: (1) the state-centric bias in e-government literature is real and consequential, (2) community institutions in SE Nigeria have demonstrable governance capacity, and (3) a polycentric lens offers a better analytical framework for understanding digital governance in the Global South.
