# Manuscript Outline: community-governance

**Title:** Beyond State-Centric E-Government: Community Institutions as Engines of Digital Transformation in Southeast Nigeria

## 1. Introduction
- **The Gap:** Digital governance literature focuses on state agencies (NITDA, e-government portals).
- **The Reality:** In SE Nigeria, town unions and community associations perform core governance.
- **The Thesis:** Recognizing these institutions as primary governance actors is essential for inclusive digital transformation.
- **Contribution:** A theoretical framework combining polycentricity and legitimacy.

## 2. Related Work: The Blind Spot of State-Centricity
- Review of e-government success metrics in the Global South.
- Critical evaluation of the "informal institution" label in digital governance.
- Mapping the digital invisibility of SE Nigerian community governance.

## 3. Theoretical Framework: Polycentricity and Legitimacy
- **3.1 Polycentric Governance (Ostrom):** Defining autonomous, nested governance units.
- **3.2 Legitimacy Theory (Beetham, Tyler):** Legal validity, moral justification, and procedural fairness.
- **3.3 Institutional Hybridity:** The intersection of customary authority and digital bureaucratic logic.

## 4. Empirical Grounding: The Infrastructure of Community Governance
- **Case 1:** Town Unions as the "Fourth Tier" (Anambra/Enugu state context).
- **Case 2:** Women's Associations and Dual-Sex Political Systems.
- **Case 3:** ROSCAs (Esusu) as financial coordination primitives.

## 5. Discussion: Designing for Recognition
- From bypassed to recognized: Integration pathways.
- Design principles for community-layer digital infrastructure.
- Risks: Elite capture and over-formalization.

## 6. Conclusion
- Summary of findings.
- Policy recommendations for NITDA and regional governors.
- Future work.
