# Section 3: Design Principles — The Artifact-First Paradigm

This section outlines the core design principles of the "Artifact-First" paradigm for community governance. We define an **Artifact-First** system as one where the creation of a verifiable digital record (the artifact) is not a byproduct of coordination, but the primary mechanism through which trust and authority are established.

## 3.1 Principle 1: Receipts Over Recall
The foundational principle is the transition from "social recall" to "artifact-based evidence." In traditional community governance, decisions are valid if members "remember" them. In an Artifact-First system, a decision is only institutionally valid if it is anchored in a digital receipt that is accessible to all members. This eliminates the "trust gap" created by conflicting memories.

## 3.2 Principle 2: The Duty to Document
Following ISO 15489, we propose that institutional trust is a function of the "Duty to Document." Every governance action—a meeting, a treasury disbursement, a land dispute resolution—must have a corresponding digital artifact with specific metadata (Purpose, Timestamp, Signatories). The system must be designed to make this documentation a required "service-level enforcement" (DD-03) rather than an optional administrative task.

## 3.3 Principle 3: Non-Repudiable Anchors
Trust in digital records requires that they be non-repudiable. Once a community decision is "Closed" or "Finalized" in the system, it must be anchored in a way that prevents post-hoc alteration (e.g., via cryptographic hashing or on-chain registration). This prevents the "Recall Trap" where leaders attempt to change decisions after the meeting has concluded.

## 3.4 Principle 4: Horizontal Transparency
Unlike state-centric systems that focus on vertical accountability (reporting to the state), Artifact-First systems focus on horizontal transparency (reporting to the community). The artifacts must be "legible" to the average community member, providing a clear "audit trail" of how decisions were reached and how funds were spent.
