# Section 2: Related Work — Records as Governance Infrastructure

The link between record-keeping and governance is well-established in formal public administration but remains under-explored in informal community governance. This section synthesizes literature from records management, legal theory, and African governance.

## 2.1 Records Management for Good Governance in Africa
Recent research in sub-Saharan Africa (Mosweu & Rakemane 2020) argues that records management is not a back-office function but is central to promoting good governance. Principles such as accountability, transparency, and the rule of law depend on the existence of reliable evidence. However, many African institutions struggle with outdated archival laws and backlogs of unprocessed records, leading to a "governance deficit." Oroma addresses this by bringing records management to the "Third Tier" of community institutions.

## 2.2 Procedural Justice and the Perception of Fairness
Tyler (2006) posited that trust in institutions is driven by "Procedural Justice"—the belief that the processes used to make decisions are fair. In informal settings, this fairness is often threatened by the lack of verifiable records, leading to accusations of bias or favoritism. The World Bank (2023) has identified records management as the "infrastructure for accountability," arguing that without verifiable records, transparency is impossible.

## 2.3 ISO 15489: Adaptability to Informal Settings
ISO 15489, the international standard for records management, defines records as "authentic, reliable, usable, and integral" (2016). While designed for formal organizations, its principles are adaptable to informal community governance. The "duty to document" and the requirement for "metadata-rich" records are critical for transitioning from recall-based systems to artifact-based systems.

## 2.4 The Trust-Artifact Gap
Despite the recognized importance of records, there is a gap in the literature regarding how *digital* records can stabilize *informal* institutions. Most records management research focuses on the "digital transition" of existing paper-based bureaucracies. Our research contributes by proposing a model for "Artifact-First" record-keeping in environments that may never have had a robust paper-based bureaucracy to begin with.
