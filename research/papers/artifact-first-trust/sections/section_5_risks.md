# Section 5: Risks — Sovereignty and Sustainability

The transition to Artifact-First governance is not without risks. This section discusses the primary challenges of implementing digital record-keeping in informal settings.

## 5.1 Community Data Sovereignty
Who owns the community's digital artifacts? If records are stored on centralized cloud platforms, the community may lose sovereignty over its own institutional memory. We propose that Artifact-First systems must prioritize **Community Sovereignty**, ensuring that the data is locally accessible and not subject to external corporate control.

## 5.2 The "Transparency Paradox"
Pozen (2018) identifies a "transparency paradox," where increased record-keeping can lead to "performative governance"—leaders focus on making the records *look* good while the actual governance remains opaque. To mitigate this, Artifact-First systems must move beyond "post-hoc reporting" to "real-time anchoring" of deliberative state transitions.

## 5.3 Digital Exclusion
The requirement for digital artifacts could potentially exclude older community members or those without high-capability devices. To be inclusive, Artifact-First systems must be "multi-modal," allowing for hybrid record-keeping where a digital officer anchors the records on behalf of the broader assembly, while ensuring that the results are verifiable through simpler, more accessible interfaces.

## 5.4 Sustainability of Digital Infrastructure
Informal institutions often lack the budget for permanent IT staff. Any digital record-keeping system must be low-maintenance and "institutionally durable"—able to survive leadership transitions and periods of technical neglect without losing the integrity of the historical records.
