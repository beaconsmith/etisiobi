## 2. Working Thesis

### Current thesis
Community institutions (town unions, ROSCAs, diaspora associations) depend on individual memory and ad-hoc records. Leadership turnover, device loss, or platform migration can destroy institutional memory. Drawing on Indigenous data sovereignty frameworks (OCAP, CARE principles) and organizational continuity planning (ISO 15489), this paper asks whether "digital sovereignty" can be operationalized at community scale: control over data custody, continuity across leadership transitions, and recoverability after disruption.

### One-sentence contribution claim
This paper adapts sovereignty and continuity concepts from Indigenous data governance and organizational records management to community-scale informal associations.

### Why this paper exists
The sovereignty literature is entirely state-level or Indigenous-peoples-level. Small community institutions face the same data custody and continuity problems but have no framework.
