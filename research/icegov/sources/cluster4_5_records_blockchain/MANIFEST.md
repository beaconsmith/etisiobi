## Key Evidence Saved

**Records Management Failures in Nigerian LGA (direct empirical evidence):**
- "Lack the culture of managing information and other relevant documents"
- Staff "lacked requisite skills, training and development" in secretarial practices
- Personnel cannot "distinguish records books for different records purposes"
- Reliance on manual filing and retrieval systems
- Gross inefficiency and lack of policy continuity

**Blockchain Governance Evidence:**
- Kenya: blockchain for property record verification and informal sector credit
- Ghana: Bitland blockchain for land registration
- World Bank FundsChain: blockchain for investment project fund tracking
- 7 African nations with full regulatory clarity for digital assets (2024)
- Only 5 of 155 reviewed articles explicitly discuss governance frameworks grounded in trust principles (Heliyon 2024) — revealing a gap

**Standards-Based Continuity Chain:**
- ISO 15489: Authenticity + Reliability + Integrity + Usability
- OAIS: Preservation + Designated Community Access
- NIST SP 800-53: Auditability controls
- RFC 6962: Append-only logs + consistency proofs
- W3C VC: Tamper-evident claims

## Critical Argument Enabled

Current blockchain governance literature focuses on supply chain, land registries, and national procurement. **No paper applies blockchain audit trails specifically to community-led cooperative governance in Africa** — the gap Oroma's Truth Layer fills. The OGI Framework is the first to measure whether community-level blockchain records meet ISO 15489-quality evidence standards.
