## The Core Paper Argument (One Paragraph)

Existing digital governance measurement frameworks — EGDI, OECD DGI, GTMI — measure what states do to citizens. They compute nationally-aggregated scores for service availability, infrastructure, and human capital, leaving community-layer governance institutions structurally invisible. In Southeast Nigeria, where town unions (Igbo Enwe Eze: Igbo has no king), esusu savings circles, and diaspora cooperatives constitute a de-facto fourth tier of government, this measurement gap is not a minor omission — it is a systematic failure to see where governance actually happens. Oroma, a Bitcoin L2 community coordination OS, converts the informal WhatsApp-screenshot governance of these institutions into a verifiable on-chain record system (Propose → Approve → Execute → Receipt → Export). This paper proposes the Oroma Governance Indicator (OGI) Framework — the first indicator set specifically designed to measure community-layer digital governance success, grounded in ISO 15489 records standards, CARE data sovereignty principles, and Ostrom's design principles for collective action — and argues that such a framework is both necessary and now tractable.
