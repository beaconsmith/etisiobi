# Evidence Policy

> Non-negotiable rules for how evidence is handled in this research system.

---

## Core Rules

### 1. No claim without source
Every non-trivial claim in any paper must be traceable to at least one source in `source_registry.md` or explicitly marked as author reasoning/conjecture.

### 2. No second-hand citation unless marked
If you know a claim only through a secondary source (e.g., "Ostrom 1990 as cited in Muwafu 2024"), mark it explicitly. Prefer primary sources.

### 3. No unverifiable quote
Every direct quote must include: exact text, author, year, and page number (or section/URL if no pagination). If page number is unavailable, note "page unavailable."

### 4. Two-support rule for major claims
Every major claim (one that the paper's argument depends on) needs at least 2 supporting sources OR 1 direct primary source with strong evidence.

### 5. Explicit marking of conjecture vs evidence
Use clear language:
- **Evidenced**: "Research shows that…" (with citation)
- **Reasoned**: "We argue that…" or "It follows that…"
- **Conjectured**: "It is plausible that…" or "Future work might explore…"

### 6. Counterargument obligation
If a claim has known opposing literature, the paper must acknowledge it. Suppressing inconvenient sources is a quality failure.

### 7. Regional evidence specificity
"Africa" is not "Nigeria." "Nigeria" is not "Southeast Nigeria." State the actual geographic scope of each source's evidence. Do not let generic regional framing substitute for specific grounding.

---

## Evidence Confidence Levels

| Level | Definition | Usage |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| **Strong** | Multiple peer-reviewed sources with direct empirical support | Can be stated confidently |
| **Moderate** | 1-2 sources with relevant evidence, or strong theoretical support | Can be stated with appropriate hedging |
| **Weak** | Single source, grey literature, or analogical reasoning only | Must be explicitly qualified |
| **None** | No source support found | Must be marked as conjecture or removed |

---

## Source Quality Hierarchy

1. Peer-reviewed journal articles and books
2. Conference papers from recognized venues (ICEGOV, CHI, CSCW, etc.)
3. International standards (ISO, IETF, W3C)
4. Dissertations and theses
5. Official policy documents and reports (World Bank, UN, government)
6. Working papers and preprints (with version noted)
7. Professional/industry reports (with credibility assessment)
8. News articles and opinion pieces (context only, not evidence)

Sources at levels 7-8 should not be primary support for major claims.
