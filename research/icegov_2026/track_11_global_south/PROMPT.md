# Research Prompt: Track 11 / Global South

## Working title:
Beyond State-Centric E-Government: Community Institutions as Engines of Digital Transformation in Southeast Nigeria

## Core thesis:
Mainstream digital governance research is too state-centric. It often overlooks the governance role of community institutions such as town unions, rotating savings circles, age grades, women’s associations, village assemblies, and diaspora associations. In Southeast Nigeria, these institutions shape coordination, legitimacy, dispute handling, development financing, and collective action. This paper argues that they should be treated as primary units of digital governance analysis rather than peripheral informal actors.

## Search Clusters to Prioritize:

- Informal institutions and hybrid governance
- Polycentric governance
- Community-led development
- Local governance in Nigeria
- Igbo political organization, town unions, civic associations
- Diaspora governance and translocal development
- Digital transformation beyond the state
- Legitimacy and non-state institutional authority

## Key Theorists and Anchors:
Elinor Ostrom, Bo Rothstein, Beetham, Tyler, Suchman, Mamdani, James Scott, North, Helmke & Levitsky, decolonial governance scholars, African local governance scholars, Nigerian community development scholars, diaspora and remittance governance scholars

## Specific Research Questions:
- What does existing literature miss when it treats governance as primarily state action?
- What concepts best explain community institutions as governance actors?
- What evidence exists from Nigeria or comparable contexts?
- What are the strongest criticisms of relying on community institutions?
- What vocabulary best frames these institutions for ICEGOV without sounding romantic or vague?
